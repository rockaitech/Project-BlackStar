The problem

Colleges and MSMEs are told to “be secure.” Nobody tells them what it costs to not be.

AICTE's constituency — Indian colleges, government departments, MSMEs — runs with no SIEM, no EDR, no CSPM, and no security staff. The tools built for enterprises assume all four. So risk stays a vague colour on a dashboard instead of a number a registrar or a CFO can act on.

  • ✕Scores, not money. “High risk” doesn't compete for budget against a new lab or a hostel block. A rupee figure does.
  • ✕Static advice. “Patch your portal” assumes the attacker stands still. Real attackers reroute the moment you close a door.
  • ✕Invented numbers. Most tools output a confident figure no one can trace to a source — and it collapses under one honest question.
  • ✕Agents required. Solutions that need software installed on every host never reach an institution with no IT team.
The question that sinks everyone
“How do you know ₹14.3 lakh is the right number?”
Every risk platform outputs a confident rupee figure. Almost none can answer this. BlackStar's answer isn't a bigger claim — it's a checkable frequency: we calibrate so a material breach lands once every N years, report the rupee figure that falls out, and show with a tornado analysis exactly which assumption the number leans on. A rupee figure is unfalsifiable. A return period, a registrar can argue with.
The solution

Three layers, from a domain name to a defensible investment.

Passive by design — input is a domain, not an agent install. Output is rupees, not a score. Every figure is computed by a tested engine, never written into a slide.

LAYER 01

Passive & active intake

Certificate-transparency discovery, then authorized active recon — DNS, port scan, HTTP fingerprint, TLS and header analysis. Real products become real CVEs via NVD, scored live by EPSS and CISA KEV. No questionnaire, no agent, no Shodan key.

LAYER 02

Bayesian attack graph

Assets become nodes; lateral-movement odds become edges. A Monte Carlo cascade over Beta-PERT loss magnitudes yields the full loss-exceedance curve — expected annual loss and its tail — never a lone point estimate. Losses sourced to IBM's India education-sector breach data.

LAYER 03

Adversarial optimizer

The defender commits to a control portfolio; the attacker best-responds along the highest-value remaining path; the solver finds the portfolio optimal against that adaptation. One exact bilevel Stackelberg equilibrium — not a greedy ranking that double-counts overlapping controls.

Why it wins

Real where it counts, and honest where it can't be.

Two independent audits found the data pipeline and the ledger genuinely real — and the maths between them worth getting exactly right. Here's what sets BlackStar apart.

Adaptive-attacker optimal

An exact bilevel Stackelberg equilibrium: enumerate every affordable portfolio, and for each, the attacker's exact best response. It even surfaces the backfire case — where the obvious “just patch the portal” advice leaves you worse off, because it reroutes the attacker through more of your assets.

Correlation-aware

Two controls covering the same asset combine on remaining risk — 1−∏(1−eᵢ) — never summed. Naive ranking double-counts and picks the wrong portfolio; we measured it overclaiming ₹10 L while under-delivering.

Genuinely stochastic

A real Monte Carlo over Beta-PERT distributions — not a deterministic PRNG wearing a distribution costume — producing a loss-exceedance curve and a real VaR₉₅, the FAIR-standard deliverable.

Sourced coefficients

Loss magnitude keyed to IBM's Cost of a Data Breach, India, education sector — ₹3,139/record — not a hand-typed constant. Likelihood comes from EPSS, a live per-CVE feed, annualized via Poisson and floored by CISA KEV.

Tamper-evident evidence

Every observation is written into a SHA-256 hash-chained ledger with independent re-verification. You can prove the risk figure was computed from evidence that hasn't been edited after the fact.

Honest by construction

Every node is labelled OBSERVED, INFERRED or MODELLED. A tornado analysis names the assumptions the answer depends on — and the recommendation stays stable across the whole plausible range.
